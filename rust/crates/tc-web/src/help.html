Tourcalc

A shared wallet for a trip. Everyone pays for whatever is convenient at the moment — one buys the tickets, another the groceries, a third the taxi — and nobody counts anything until the end. Tourcalc keeps the record and, when you are done, says who has to pay whom and how much, in as few payments as possible.

Getting started

  1. Create a tour. One tour is one trip, one flat, one project — any group of people with a common pot. + New tour on the tour list.
  2. Add the people. People → + Add person. Names only; no accounts, no invitations.
  3. Record expenses as they happen. The + Spend button is on every screen. Amount, what it was for, who paid, who it was for.
  4. At the end, open Balance. It shows the payments that square everyone up. When a payment actually happens, press Mark paid.

Nothing here is money. Tourcalc moves no funds and talks to no bank — it is a notebook that can do arithmetic.

People and shares

A shared expense is split by weight, not by headcount. A full share is 100. Someone on 50 pays for half as much of everything shared — a child, or a person who joined for half the trip. A custom 0 means they pay for nothing at all while still appearing in the list.

Weights only matter relative to each other: three people on 100 and three on 200 split exactly like 50 / 100. The People tab shows the total weight, and tapping it explains how much of a shared expense one full share carries.

Somebody can pay for others — children, a partner: their debts and credits are settled as one family. Open the person on the People tab and press + Companions (Companions · 2 once there are some): the people travelling at their expense are listed with their weights, and a row for the next appears as you type. ✕ takes somebody off the list — they stay in the tour and pay for themselves again.

People who pay for themselves but belong together — two couples, a family of adults — can be kept side by side in the lists: in the person's form, under More options, give them the same Together text, say “the Smiths”. It changes only the order; what anybody pays or gets stays the same.

A tour opens on the People tab: who owes what is what it is opened for during a trip. On top, Who pays next names whoever owes the most. When they pay the next bill for everyone, the fewest payments are left at the end; it also says how big a bill evens their debt out, and who comes after them. Its button records the expense with them as the payer. A tour being settled up opens on Balance instead, and the suggestion is not shown there.

+ Spend starts from whoever this device last recorded a new expense for — as a rule, whoever holds the phone. An expense recorded for somebody else, from “Who pays next” or the 💸 on their card, leaves that as it was.

Recording an expense

Amount
What was actually paid. In a multi-currency tour, pick the currency it was paid in.
What for
The description you will recognise later in the list.
Paid by
The person whose money left the pocket. Pre-filled with whoever you last recorded as paying.
Split between
everyone shares it by weight. Turn it off to pick particular people — it is then split between just those, still by weight. (Older expenses may carry a split equally mark: those ignore weights and divide by headcount.)
Category
Groups the statistics. A new expense arrives with the last used category already picked; while it is only a guess it is drawn with a dashed outline and the note “Last used”. Tap any category to make it a decision. On Stats, a chosen category has Show these expenses →: the Expenses tab, filtered to it — “Food” to every “Food/…”, a line under it to just that one.
Date
Defaults to today. Change it when you are catching up on yesterday.

More options

Draft
A possible expense, not a real one. It stays in the list marked draft · not counted and changes no balance — useful for “what if we rent the boat”. Ticking Still count it in the balance makes it count while keeping the label.
Colour
Marks a rare, out-of-the-ordinary expense — a tourist tax for the whole group, an unexpected fine — so it catches the eye when you scroll the list. It has no effect on any calculation.

What counts as spending

Total spent, the day totals and the summary above the list all count the same thing: an expense that has a category and is not a draft (or is a draft you told it to count).

Everything else is money that moved without being spent, and it is shown separately as uncounted:

settling up
Someone handing money to someone else inside the group. It is a transfer, not a purchase — counting it would count the same dinner twice. An expense with no category is treated as exactly this.
draft
Not real yet, so not spent yet.

Tapping the uncounted figure lists what is in it and why. This is the single most common source of “the total looks wrong” — it is usually right, and it is usually a payback.

Balances and settling up

Paid
What this person put in — everything they paid for, for anyone.
Charged
What their own part of the spending cost — their share of everything that was shared with them.
Balance
Paid minus Charged. + means they put in more than their share cost and the group owes them; − means their share cost more and they pay the difference; 0 — neither; while the tour is being settled up it reads settled. Hover over it, or have it read out, and it says so in words.

All three open an itemised list when tapped, whatever the settings say.

Who pays whom

The Balance tab does not simply list debts — it works out the shortest set of payments that brings everyone to zero, so five people rarely need more than four transfers. Nothing on that list has happened yet. When the money really changes hands, press Mark paid: it records a payback (it appears in the list as payback) and both balances move towards zero.

Small leftovers are ignored on purpose. Settings → Ignore debts smaller than (49 by default) decides what counts as “already square”, so nobody is asked to transfer three coins.

Companions: paying for someone else

A child, a partner, a friend who is out of cash — someone whose share you settle yourself. In the person’s card, set Paid for by to whoever covers them. They keep their own weight and their own share of every expense, but they hand nothing over: their debt is folded into their payer’s.

That is why a covered person shows a grey −… rather than a red one, and why their payer can show two numbers: the settlement total, and own — their personal part of it, before the people they cover. Transfers inside a family are listed separately, under Inside families.

Several currencies

Two different things are easy to confuse, so they live in two different places:

currencies
The main currency of the tour and the rates. It is a property of the tour: changing it changes what everyone sees.
show amounts in
A switch for you alone. It converts what is on your screen and changes nothing in the tour; this device remembers it for each tour, and reset beside it goes back to the main currency.

Each currency has a rate against the reference — the cheapest currency of the tour, the one the others are worked out from: “1 EUR = 117.5 RSD”. Type it with a point or a comma; four figures are plenty. A new currency starts level with the reference until you give it its rate. Naming one, type its code, its name or its country, in English or Russian — “Belar”, “рубль”, “Bangla” — and pick it from the list; if nothing fits, the dialog says so.

Every expense stays attached to the currency it was entered in. Renaming a currency is safe — the amounts keep their meaning. Removing one converts the expenses entered in it into the cheapest real currency left (not into chips), at the tour's rates, and the dialog says how many before you save.

A currency with cents is entered and shown with a decimal part — 3.50 — and either a point or a comma will do when you type it. New currencies worth thirty times the cheapest one or more (a euro against dinars) start with cents; any currency can be switched in the currencies dialog, and every figure stays what it was. An older “EURc” beside a euro can be folded into it there too.

Today's rates for all sets every currency at once: add them all, press it, and each gets the day's market rate, the cheapest of them by the market becoming the reference. today's rate beside a currency does the same for that one. Rates are rounded to four figures, since nobody settles a trip to the last dinar; nothing is saved until you press Save, and a line under each says what its rate was. A currency the source does not know — casino chips, say — keeps its rate against the rest. The rates come from ExchangeRate-API, once a day; your browser asks it directly, so that service sees your IP address.

Menu and shopping

If the group cooks for itself, switch on Plan the food in the tour's settings (the pencil by its name) and a Menu tab appears. It has two views.

Plan is the days, each with breakfast, lunch and dinner. Pick a dish from the list; the On every day row puts one dish on that meal on every day at once, and “— nothing —” is a meal eaten out. − and + change the number of days, which starts as the tour's length. Below is what is bought every day whatever is cooked: vegetables, bread, water, wine.

Above the days are Starts on, First day and Last day. With a start date the days are called by their dates: “Friday, 13 November”. Arriving on Friday for dinner and leaving on Sunday after breakfast, choose “from dinner” and “until breakfast”, and the meals before and after become “— nothing —”. What is bought every day is counted from the plan: “every day” is the days something is cooked, “with dinner” (as wine is) the days with a dinner. In that example that is three days of juice and water and two evenings of wine.

Shopping is worked out from the plan and the people: a weight of 100 is one portion, a child at 35 a third of one. Wine and the like is one each for everybody at the tour's most common weight (the “×100” on People), juice for everybody else. Under each product it says what it is for: “Plov ×1 · Fish ×1”, or “every day”.

It is one list, and each product says where it is bought: market, supermarket, delivery. Sort it as in the catalogue, by name, by place, by who buys, or by category, or with what is not bought yet on top; Group makes a card per place or per category. Each product has who buys and pays; All to in a heading puts one person on all the products of the list or of the place. Tick things off as they are bought. Record by a product opens an expense with that person, a description and a category, for the amount; several products off one receipt go into one expense with Record the bought as one expense at the foot of the place. A recorded product shows its expense's amount (“in one receipt” when it is several products'); delete the expense and the product is unrecorded again.

Who am I is chosen on this phone: with it you can hide other people's shopping and see only yours and what nobody has taken yet, and every product not yours has a Mine button. The search above the list looks at the name, the place, the person and the dishes; each product also says its place and who it is for — everybody, the adults or the children.

The Catalogue is what all this is worked out from; its search unfolds a folded section when the match is in it. Dishes: a name, which meals it can be (several at once — sandwiches for breakfast and for lunch), and what goes in: a product and how much of it per portion — one from the list, or a new one typed into the blank row at the end with its unit (pearl barley, g, 50); it joins the products, bought at the supermarket (or the first place there is) by everybody, and a name the list already has is that product. Every day is what is bought daily whatever is cooked, and for which days: every day, or with breakfast, lunch or dinner. Products: what they are counted in (g, ml, pcs, or a unit of your own — “btl”, “can”: a portion may be a fraction of one, the shopping is whole ones; units the tour's products already have are offered in the list), where they are bought, and who for — everybody by weight, or one each for the adults or the children, and a category (“Drinks”, “Alcohol”). A new place or category is typed right there in the product; places are renamed under Places. A product in a dish or on the daily list cannot be deleted until it is taken out there, and it says where it is; so with a place things are bought at. A dish with nothing in it (“Restaurant”) is never put on the plan by itself, only when chosen. A deleted dish leaves the plan: its meals become “— nothing —”.

Under the catalogue, Save as template keeps its dishes, products, places and daily list for the access code — as a tour of its own, “📋 Menu template”, listed apart under Menu templates; saving again replaces it, and its versions keep the old one. A tour under that code that switches its menu on starts from the template instead of the built-in one, and Take from template gives any tour the template's catalogue: the plan keeps the dishes the template has, the rest of its meals are planned again. The template is edited like any catalogue, by opening it. There is one per code: a copy of it is an ordinary tour, and deleting it asks twice.

Sharing a tour

share link in the tour header copies an invite link to the clipboard. Opening that link logs the other person in by itself — no code to type, no account to create.

share link copies the tour's link, and for a few seconds offers or to the … tab — the tab that is open: the same invitation, landing on the menu, say, instead of wherever the tour opens. The address bar names the open tab too (…/tour/…/menu), for somebody already signed in.

The link carries the access code of the tour, and that code opens every tour created under it — not only the one you meant to share. Whoever gets the link can also edit everything they can see. Treat it like a key, and use a separate code for anything you do not want in the same bundle.

Typing that same code on the sign-in screen is the other way in.

Turning on Push notifications in the settings adds a bell to each tour, so the browser can tell you when somebody else changes it.

Offline and refreshing

The tour is kept on the device, so it opens and edits without a connection; changes are sent when there is one. The line under the tour name says which copy you are looking at — from server or local copy — and how old it is.

The button asks the server and then says what happened, plainly:

new data received
Somebody had changed something; you are looking at it now.
server has nothing newer
The request went through and came back with the same tour.
server did not answer
No connection. What you see is the local copy, and it is still safe to edit.

When the numbers look wrong

“Total spent is less than what we actually spent”
Paybacks and drafts are not spending. Tap the uncounted figure next to the total to see exactly what was left out.
“This expense is not in the statistics”
It has no category. An expense without one is treated as settling up.
“Nobody owes anything, but we clearly do”
Debts under Ignore debts smaller than are treated as settled. Lower it in the settings if you really do split coins.
“One person shows two different debts”
They pay for someone. The large figure is what they hand over in total, own is their personal part.
“I do not recognise these amounts”
Check show amounts in in the tour header — you may be looking at another currency.
“Somebody changed something and I cannot see it”
Press next to the tour name. If it says the server did not answer, you are offline and looking at the local copy.
