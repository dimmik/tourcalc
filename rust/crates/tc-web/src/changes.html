Change log

What changed in Tourcalc, when, and why — newest first. It starts on 8 September 2026, when the app began to be rewritten from C#/Blazor into Rust; the first days are told in broad strokes. Your tours, your access code and your links did not change: both versions read the same database.

1 October 2026

  • Where a product is bought is said in the shopping, by whoever buys it - type “Lidl” or pick a place already said; the catalogue's place is only where it starts. The place was the catalogue's, one for everybody, while Sasha takes the beer at Lidl and somebody else has theirs delivered. Grouping by place follows what the shopping says.
  • The shopping can be grouped by who buys - a card per person, and one of what nobody has taken yet. Sorting by buyer kept everyone's products in one long list, so sharing out the rest meant scrolling past what was already taken.
  • With two access codes on one device, a copy or a template goes under the tour's own code. The server filed every new tour under the first code the device held, so a copy of a tour from the second code - or its menu template - turned up under the first.
  • A day of the plan can have a word of its own - “Friday, 13 November, day 1 - arrival”: “✎ note” by the day's name writes it. Before, a day was its date or number, and what the day was for lived in somebody's head.
  • Menu templates in the tour list say whose they are - “for: Danube, Shipka and 5 more” - and their section is folded until opened. With two access codes on one device there were two templates of one name, and no telling which was which.
  • “Saved here, waiting to be sent” appears only when it is true for a while - after five seconds of waiting. On a good network the edit reaches the server sooner, and the line flickered after every save.
  • A meal can have several dishes - plov, and a salad, and mulled wine: “+ another dish” under a meal in the plan adds one, a cross takes it off, and the shopping counts each. Before, a meal was one dish, and a salad on the side had to go on the daily list or nowhere.
  • The new-product row in a dish offers the catalogue's products, and one picked brings its unit, which then cannot be changed there. Before, nothing was offered, and a product typed in the wrong unit - rice in pieces - had to be caught by an error.
  • Dishes are added by the list, copied, and the catalogue can be cleared. “+ Dish” takes several names at once, each with its meals, to be filled in later; “make a copy” under a dish puts one right under it, open for changing; “Clear the dishes” and “Clear the daily list” empty just that, and “Clear the catalogue” empties dishes, products and the daily list, keeping the places. Before, dishes came one at a time, a variant meant typing the whole dish again, and starting from nothing meant deleting everything by hand.
  • Tours that had gone missing are back, with their versions. A tour the old app had saved with an expense that has no category could not be read by the new server, which answered as if the tour did not exist: it dropped out of the list, and its link said “no such tour”. Nothing was deleted.
  • A menu can be taken to another access code: “Copy menu” under the catalogue puts its dishes, products, places and daily list on the clipboard, and “Paste menu” in a tour under the other code puts them in place of its catalogue. Before, a group's menu stayed under its code, and the only way across was a whole tour's JSON - people and expenses included.

30 September 2026

  • A dish's meals are chips to press, like an expense's payer and category: outlined when not chosen, the meal's own coloured badge when chosen. They were pills with a checkbox inside, which nothing else in the app has.
  • A new product can be typed right into a dish - its name, unit and how much, in the blank row under the ingredients - and joins the catalogue when the dish is saved: at the supermarket, for everybody. Before, a solyanka with pearl barley meant leaving the dish, adding the barley under Products and coming back. The daily list has the same row.
  • “Share link” can share a tab: it copies the tour's link as before, then offers “or to the Menu tab” - the tab that is open - whose link signs the newcomer in straight onto it. The address names the tab as well — /tour/…/menu, /balance. Before, every link landed on the tour's first tab, and saying “open the menu” was the only way to point at it. Switching tabs does not add steps to Back.
  • A menu template for the access code. “Save as template” under the catalogue keeps its dishes, products, places and daily list; a tour that switches its menu on starts from them, and “Take from template” gives them to any tour. Before, every new tour started from the built-in catalogue, and a group's own dishes had to be typed again each trip. The template is a tour of its own, listed apart under “Menu templates” and opening on its menu; there is one per code - a copy of it is an ordinary tour - and deleting it asks twice.
  • Edits waiting for the network survive an update of the app. If one of them was saved by a version whose edits this one cannot read, the others are still sent, and the tour says that one was not. Before, a single such edit made the device forget everything it had not sent yet, expenses included, without a word.
  • Menu changes are in the tour's versions - “Menu: dinner day 2 Fish -> Plov; bought Lamb” - so a changed plan can be found and put back. They still send nobody a notification: a tick on the shopping list is not news.
  • The tour's settings list their switches one under another, each with its note under its words. Side by side, the third switch wrapped on its own and the notes ran into the next switch.
  • The catalogue says its amounts are for one portion - by the Dishes and Every day headings - and, under the dishes, what a portion comes to in this tour. The line about the tour's portions named wine and juice whatever the menu had; it now says “adults” and “children”, as the products do.
  • A save no longer throws the page to its end. With the tour's header scrolled away, ticking a product or taking it on yourself landed the reader at the bottom of the list; the page now stays where it was.
  • Products have categories, and the shopping groups by them - or by place, or not at all. A new category or place is typed right in the product, the places are renamed in their own section, and something that cannot be deleted says where it is used instead of “somewhere”. The starter menu has a Restaurant for meals eaten out.
  • The shopping and the catalogue have a search, and a folded catalogue section opens when the match is in it. With “Who am I” chosen, a Mine button takes a product in one tap, and every product says who it is for - everybody, adults or children.
  • The shopping is one list, each product saying where it is bought, and it sorts - by name, place, who buys, or what is still to buy on top; “Split by place” gives the cards per place back. A new menu starts from a catalogue a group calibrated on a real trip: wine and beer per adult, juice and water in packs and jugs. Units already used in the tour are offered when a product's unit is chosen.
  • A product can have a unit of its own - “btl” for wine, “can” for beer - bought in whole ones: wine is half a bottle each with dinner, and the shopping says 8 btl rather than 5.6 l. The catalogue is easier to find your way in: dishes, the daily list and the products fold and each has its own background, and breakfast, lunch and dinner have a colour each, here and on the plan.
  • The menu knows when the trip starts and ends. Give the first day's date and the days read “Friday, 13 November”; choose “from dinner” and “until breakfast” and the meals outside the trip are empty. Daily items count by the plan - wine goes with dinner - so a Friday to Sunday weekend is two evenings of wine, not three.
  • “Saved here, waiting to be sent” floats above the “+ Spend” button instead of sitting above the tabs. It showed for half a second after every save and pushed the whole page down and back up - a tick on the shopping list made the list jump.
  • The menu's catalogue can be edited: dishes (their name, which meals they can be - several at once - and what goes into them per portion), what is bought every day, and the products (unit, where to buy, who for). Until now the menu came as it was, and a dish nobody in the group eats could only be left off the plan.
  • A Menu tab: what the group cooks on which day, and what to buy for it. Switch on “Plan the food” in the tour's settings. Pick breakfast, lunch and dinner for each day; the shopping is worked out by the people's weights and says what each thing is for; it is split into market, supermarket and delivery, and every product has who buys and pays for it, a tick, and a button that records it as an expense - one product, or a whole receipt. Until now the food list lived in a chat, separately from the money.
  • On People, the balance sits in the middle of its column, heading and all. It was pushed to the left, with its heading a little lower than Paid and Charged beside it.
  • A zero balance reads “0” during the trip, in Mini too. It read “settled” (a tick in Mini), which sounded like “done with this person” while tomorrow's dinner was still to come. “Settled” is kept for a tour being settled up, where it means exactly that.

29 September 2026

  • The history names everybody added at once. Two companions added together showed in Versions as “P 'Эмма' added”, and Артём seemed never to have joined — the line named only the last of them, as the old app did. It is “P 'Эмма', 'Артём' added” now, and the same for people removed together.
  • “+ Companions” instead of “+ Pays for…”. The button reads as whom a person takes along — children, a partner, a friend travelling at their expense — and says how many once there are some: “Companions · 2” — as do the person's details, the edit waiting to be sent and the help. “Pays for…” said what it does to the numbers and left a newcomer guessing what to add there.
  • “Together”: people who belong together stand side by side. The old app could keep, say, two couples next to each other in the list even though each paid for themselves, but the field for it had gone from its forms. It is back in the person's form under “More options”: the same text for several people keeps them together in every list. Nothing is counted differently. Mini now sorts people by name too, as the old app's did; it used to list them in the order they were added.

28 September 2026

  • “— платит Валечка” under a family in Russian. The line under somebody who pays for others read “Олег за них платит Валечка” — “them” even for a single child. It is “Олег — платит Валечка” now, whether there is one of them or three.
  • Everybody's weight is on their line again. Only weights that differed from the usual one were marked, so a line with no “×100” read as a person with no weight at all, and finding out meant opening them. Now each line says it; somebody paying for others still shows what the family weighs.

27 September 2026

  • The newest change is shown on its own. Finding out what had changed meant opening the whole log. Now the latest entry stands above it, and the rest is one tap away as before.
  • A balance is a sign: +6 870, −3 205. “gets 6 870” and “owes 3 205” took room a row did not have — on a phone on its side there was space for the numbers but not the words. The sign is the balance itself, put in less charged: + comes to them, − is theirs to pay, and nothing either way reads “settled”. The words are still there on hover and for screen readers. It is a chip in every layout, the table row included — the figure a row is looked at for; and on a touch screen paid and charged are underlined with dots, since there is no hover to show they open their lists.
  • People is one list, a line per person. Folded people stood three to a row, and names were cut to “Va…”; opening one swapped its line for a different card — the name moved, the weight changed places, 💸 vanished from under the finger. Now there is one line at any width, the name wraps instead of being cut, and opening adds the details below it. From a phone on its side the line is a table row, no thicker than on a phone held upright: paid, charged and balance in columns, each on one line; on a wide screen “+ Pays for…” and Edit join them, Edit in one column down the list, and an open person's Delete ends the line of their details rather than taking one of its own. A weight is marked only where it differs from everybody else's — and with as many on 50 as on 100, it is the 50s that are marked, whatever order people were added in. The “Compact” button is gone — there is nothing left for it to switch.
  • A tour opens on its people. It opened on the expenses, and during a trip the first thing done there was switching to People to see who owes what. A tour being settled up still opens on Balance.
  • “Who pays next”. At a restaurant anybody could pay, and whoever did was a guess — often one that made the settle-up at the end longer. On top of People there is now the person who owes the most, how big a bill for everyone evens that out, and who comes after them. Its button records the expense in their name: “Dima, you pay, I'll put it down”.
  • “+ Spend” starts from whoever this phone records for. It always started from the first person in the list, so whoever held the phone picked themselves out every time. Now it is whoever this device last recorded a new expense for — as the old app did — or, before that, whoever paid the latest one. An expense recorded for somebody else, from their card or from “Who pays next”, does not change it.
  • From a category on Stats to its expenses. “Food 2 500” said how much, and finding what it was meant going to Expenses and picking the chips one by one. Now a chosen category has “Show these expenses →”: “Food” brings up every “Food/…”, a line under it just that one. In Mini, a line of Stats does the same when tapped.
  • “↑” back to the top. Far down a long list of expenses, getting back to the search and the filters was a long swipe. Past two screens a round ↑ appears at the bottom left.

26 September 2026

  • “+ Pays for…” on a person. Adding children or a partner somebody pays for meant “Add person”, a name, “Paid by” picked — once for each of them. Now the person's own button opens whom they pay for: a row per person, name and weight, the next row appearing as you type, saved together.
  • A form that opens is ready to type in. “Add a person”, a new expense, editing a tour or a person, the currencies, a new tour, the sign-in screen: the cursor was nowhere, and the name started being typed into nothing. Now it is in the first box — after the text already there, when there is some.
  • Share links from several companies add up again. In the app, opening a share link added its tours to the ones you already saw — the hiking group, the work group, the sports group, all in one list. Here each link replaced the last, and the other companies' tours disappeared until their link was opened again. Now a link adds its code to the ones you have, as before.
  • A new tour starts in euros. It used to start with “coin”, a stand-in nearly everybody renamed first thing — usually to the euro. Now the euro is there from the start, with cents; a tour that counts in something else renames it as before.
  • A currency's code says what it is. EUR needs no explaining, ALL does. Under each currency in the currencies dialog, beside “show amounts in”, and under an expense's amount, the code now comes with its name and country: “Albanian lek · Albania”.
  • Renaming a currency by retyping its name keeps it. Clearing the box to type a new name made the last currency in the list disappear for a moment, and typed again it came back as a new currency at a default worth: saved like that, its expenses were converted into another currency and every rate against it was off. The currency now stays what it was while its name is being typed, and one left without a name is not saved away — the dialog asks for a name, or for ✕ to remove it.
  • A currency can be found by its name or country. Adding one meant knowing its code — the Belarusian rouble had to be looked up to learn it was BYN. Now the name box suggests every currency the rates know as you type its code, its name or its country, in English or Russian, and says so when nothing fits.
  • The currencies dialog shows rates. Each currency had a bare “worth” on a scale of its own — the euro at 1 175 000 — which only meant something next to the others, and the dialog kept asking to multiply them all by ten. Now a currency reads “1 EUR = 117.5 RSD”, against the cheapest currency of the tour, and the numbers behind it are the dialog's business.
  • The first expense of a tour gets a category. In an empty tour there was nothing to guess the category from, so a new expense opened with none; saved without a look, it counted as a payback between people and was left out of the money spent. When there is only one category to offer, a new expense now starts in it.
  • “Today's rates for all”. Setting several currencies meant pressing each one's button, and a small base kept asking to multiply every worth first. Now one button sets them all: the cheapest by the market becomes 10 000 (more, if a currency with cents needs its two zeros) and the rest follow; chips keep their worth against the rest.
  • “Today's rate” no longer hangs or breaks the page. On a bad network it waited indefinitely, and closing the dialog before the answer came threw an error. It now gives up after ten seconds, and an answer to a closed dialog is dropped.
  • Smaller fixes around currencies. A removed currency's expenses go into real money, not into chips; an expense edited offline keeps its amount if the currency's cents were switched meanwhile; a currency added offline is not added twice when the save is retried; the dialog says when the debt threshold turns into cents; the text pages label an expense with its own currency; “Keep as is” is now “Don't multiply”.
  • A currency just added keeps its “today's rate”. At the default worth it could be the cheapest in the tour, and was then taken for the one the others are worked out from — so it had no button at all. Only the tour's own currencies are that now, and that one says so where the button would be.
  • Removing a currency after changing the worths keeps its expenses right. When the worths were rescaled — multiplied for “today's rate”, or a dinar retyped from 100 to 1 000 — and a currency removed in the same save, its expenses were converted at the wrong scale: a 410-dinar coffee became 41.
  • Amounts with cents in version lines and notifications. They showed the stored hundredths: “44 (4400 Eur)” for 44.00 €. They now read 44.00, and a new worth no longer adds “Currency: Eur -> Eur” to every changed expense.
  • A new tour starts at 10 000, and so does a currency added to one, so that “today's rate” comes out to four figures without offering to multiply.

25 September 2026

  • “Today's rate” beside each currency. A currency's worth had to be looked up and typed by hand, and was usually a few weeks old by the end of a trip. Now a button fills it in from the day's market rate, against the cheapest currency of the tour, rounded to four figures; nothing is saved until Save, and a line says what the worth was. When the cheapest worth is too small for four figures, or a currency with cents would not end in two zeros (the lev at 60 090 against the dinar at 1 000), the dialog offers to multiply every worth first and then fills in today's rate for all of them. A new currency now starts at 10 000 rather than 100, so there is room for the four figures and the two zeros without multiplying.
  • “Show amounts in” changes only your screen. Picking a currency there used to save the tour: it made a version, told everybody subscribed that the amounts were now in another currency, and moved the tour's main currency for all. Now it is remembered on this device, for that tour, and changes nothing else; “main — EUR · reset” beside it says what the tour itself is in. The main currency is set in the currencies dialog.
  • Currencies with cents. Amounts were whole numbers only, so 3.50 € could not be written, and tours grew a second currency, “EURc”, to hold the cents. A currency can now have cents of its own: it is entered and shown with a decimal part — 3.50 or 3,50, either separator works — and new currencies worth thirty times the cheapest one start with cents. Switching an existing currency keeps every figure, and an old EURc can be folded into its euro.
  • Removing a currency converts its expenses. They used to be quietly read in the tour's main currency, and every total changed without a word. Now they go into the cheapest currency left, at the tour's rates, and the dialog says so before saving.
  • A version's time is on your own clock. The list of a tour's versions printed the time as the server stored it, in Moscow time, so for somebody at UTC+2 a change made half an hour ago was stamped half an hour in the future. New versions are stored in UTC, and every version — old ones included — is shown in your time zone.

24 September 2026

  • “Copied — the JSON is in the clipboard.” It said “on the clipboard”, which is what the documentation writes; people asked say “in”.
  • Settings fit a phone. Each setting put its explanation beside its control, and on a narrow screen the text shrank into a column three words wide and ten lines tall. Now the name and the control share a line, the explanation is one sentence under them, and more opens the rest — less folds it back.

23 September 2026

  • The app speaks Russian as well as English. It picks the first of your browser's languages it knows and falls back to English; Settings → Language changes it for this device. Help is translated too. Notifications, the lines in a tour's history and this change log stay in English, and so do the names you gave your tours, people and categories.
  • Balances and people say what currency they are in on a tour with several currencies. The header said “65 910 RSD” while the cards below it said “owes 47 124” and “gets 64 748” with no unit. Every amount on Balance and People now carries it — “owes 47 124 RSD” — in Full; Mini names it once above each list. A tour with a single currency still shows plain numbers, as before.
  • This change log. What changed and why used to live only in the developers' notes; a reader who found something looking different today had nobody to ask.
  • “Copied” goes away by itself. Copying a tour's JSON said so in the red error box, and kept saying it until something else went wrong. It is now a green line that disappears after a few seconds; the share button's “link copied” turns back into “share link” the same way.

22 September 2026

  • Somebody who is owed money is no longer called “settled”. Dividing expenses into whole cents leaves crumbs, and a two-cent crumb could put a person who was owed 15 628 on the paying side — and then, since nobody chases two cents, they showed as “settled” right next to a payment addressed to them. Which side somebody is on is now decided with the same Ignore debts smaller than threshold the screen uses to hide small amounts. This is the one place where the figures deliberately differ from the old app: by the crumbs, and no more.
  • The tour list says how settling up is going. A tour being settled up shows an amber settling up with what is still to be paid, or a green all square once nothing is left. A tour that is not being settled up shows neither, whatever its balances: until the tour is over, being even is only a moment, not a result.
  • Archiving only tidies the list. An archived tour that is being settled up used to open on its expenses; it now opens on the balances like any other tour being settled up. Inside a tour the archive flag changes nothing.

21 September 2026

  • The header says when this version was built, in your own time zone, in place of the word “latest” — the colour already said whether it is current.
  • The list's total is what the tour cost. It used to add up everything everybody paid out, paybacks included, so it disagreed with the tour's own header.
  • “Everyone is settled up” says what is left when something is. It could sit above a balance with somebody collecting 70 — both true, because payments below the threshold are hidden. The card now names those small payments, and a button lists them with Mark paid for anyone who wants to square the pennies.
  • Searching the tour list finds archived tours too.
  • A tour no longer appears twice in the list. A tour stored in two documents was counted twice and the list kept loading “more” of the same tour.
  • Very old tours can be saved again. A tour from 2022 had no saved-state marker, so every save looked like somebody else had saved first and was refused forever.
  • The line under the tour name says which kind of trouble it was: no answer, the tour is not on the server, the server refused the change, or the server is in trouble — instead of “the server did not answer” for all four.

20 September 2026

  • The installed app opens from what the phone already has. Opening it after a while could show “Starting…” and give up: the saved copy was there but never used, so everything was downloaded again over a sleepy mobile connection. Now a slow network gets 2.5 seconds, and then the saved copy opens while the new one loads in the background.
  • The header says whether this browser has the current version, and one click fetches it when it does not.
  • Escape closes a form, and closing a new one does not lose it. Checking something elsewhere — who paid last time, how the hotel is spelled — meant closing the form and typing it all again. An unsaved new expense or person now waits on the device for two hours and comes back in the next blank form, with an offer to start blank instead.
  • The People tab keeps what you opened and typed when somebody else's edit arrives.
  • The tour list is newest first again, and says what it is sorted by.
  • Logging out forgets this device's copies of your tours; a login that merely expired keeps them.
  • Long lists load in pages from the database, so a large account's list and a tour's history open quickly.

18–19 September 2026

  • Tapping a notification opens the tour it is about, even when the app is already open somewhere else.
  • Switching between Full and Mini keeps you on the tab you were reading.
  • A person who still pays for, or shares, an expense cannot be deleted. It used to delete the expenses they paid for and turn “only for them” into “for everybody”. Now it says which expenses hold them.
  • An expired login asks for the code again instead of failing quietly; a deleted tour stays deleted rather than being brought back by a waiting edit.
  • The list says when edits are waiting for a tour it does not show, and links to it.
  • The compact People view is remembered for each tour on this device; it used to switch off with every edit.

16–17 September 2026

  • An open tour notices when somebody else changes it, and shows the change without a reload; changed rows light up for a moment. How often it checks is a setting.
  • Edits made offline are sent as soon as the network is back, wherever in the app you are, not only on the next edit.
  • An expense unfolds to show who it is for and how much each person carries — in Full and in Mini.
  • An expense for some of the people is shared by weight again.
  • Two expenses that had the same id are two expenses again. Saving one of them — without changing anything — overwrote the other.
  • Notifications read like a person wrote them, and the tour list rings the bell for tours this device follows.

13–15 September 2026

  • Help and Settings have a way back to the tour you came from, instead of the list and then the tour again.
  • Paybacks look like paybacks: green, and transfers inside a family cyan, in both lists, as in the old app.
  • The expense form and adding a person work the way they did in the old app.
  • Saving an expense no longer forgets what you were looking at.
  • The app can be installed on a phone's home screen as an app with its own icon, and says plainly when a browser will not allow it.
  • A page that cannot start says so and offers a reload, instead of staying blank.
  • The beta has a “b” on its icon, so the beta and the real one are not confused on a home screen.

10–11 September 2026

  • Mini: the same tours in a dense interface, one line per thing, for small screens and long lists.
  • Explain: every figure opens into how it was worked out — what was added, what was left out and why. Always on.
  • Stats with a chart you can click into, by category and by person.
  • Tour settings, currencies, history with restore, Mark paid, cloning, copying a tour as JSON, and two settings: the small-debt threshold and the accent colour.
  • A tour opens where it is needed: on the balances while it is being settled up, on the expenses otherwise.
  • Moving between screens no longer reloads the app — about seven times faster.
  • This help page.

8–9 September 2026 — the rewrite

  • The same app, rewritten in Rust. The arithmetic was rebuilt first and checked against the old app tour by tour, to the cent; then the server, which the old app could not tell from its own; then this interface.
  • One calculation everywhere. The screen, the server, the text pages and the explanations all use the same code, so they cannot disagree.
  • About eleven times smaller to download than the old app — 360 KB instead of 4 MB.
  • Works offline. Edits made without a network are kept on the device and sent later; if somebody else changed the tour meanwhile, both sets of changes are kept rather than one refused.
  • Two people saving at once can no longer both “win”: one save is refused and re-applied on top of the other.
  • A plain-text version at /t for browsers without JavaScript and for very bad connections, and push notifications.
