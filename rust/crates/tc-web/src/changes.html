Change log

What changed in Tourcalc, when, and why — newest first. It starts on 8 September 2026, when the app began to be rewritten from C#/Blazor into Rust; the first days are told in broad strokes. Your tours, your access code and your links did not change: both versions read the same database.

23 September 2026

  • The app speaks Russian as well as English. It picks the first of your browser's languages it knows and falls back to English; Settings → Language changes it for this device. Help is translated too. Notifications, the lines in a tour's history and this change log stay in English, and so do the names you gave your tours, people and categories.
  • Balances and people say what currency they are in on a tour with several currencies. The header said “65 910 RSD” while the cards below it said “owes 47 124” and “gets 64 748” with no unit. Every amount on Balance and People now carries it — “owes 47 124 RSD” — in Full; Mini names it once above each list. A tour with a single currency still shows plain numbers, as before.
  • This change log. What changed and why used to live only in the developers' notes; a reader who found something looking different today had nobody to ask.
  • “Copied” goes away by itself. Copying a tour's JSON said so in the red error box, and kept saying it until something else went wrong. It is now a green line that disappears after a few seconds; the share button's “link copied” turns back into “share link” the same way.

22 September 2026

  • Somebody who is owed money is no longer called “settled”. Dividing expenses into whole cents leaves crumbs, and a two-cent crumb could put a person who was owed 15 628 on the paying side — and then, since nobody chases two cents, they showed as “settled” right next to a payment addressed to them. Which side somebody is on is now decided with the same Ignore debts smaller than threshold the screen uses to hide small amounts. This is the one place where the figures deliberately differ from the old app: by the crumbs, and no more.
  • The tour list says how settling up is going. A tour being settled up shows an amber settling up with what is still to be paid, or a green all square once nothing is left. A tour that is not being settled up shows neither, whatever its balances: until the tour is over, being even is only a moment, not a result.
  • Archiving only tidies the list. An archived tour that is being settled up used to open on its expenses; it now opens on the balances like any other tour being settled up. Inside a tour the archive flag changes nothing.

21 September 2026

  • The header says when this version was built, in your own time zone, in place of the word “latest” — the colour already said whether it is current.
  • The list's total is what the tour cost. It used to add up everything everybody paid out, paybacks included, so it disagreed with the tour's own header.
  • “Everyone is settled up” says what is left when something is. It could sit above a balance with somebody collecting 70 — both true, because payments below the threshold are hidden. The card now names those small payments, and a button lists them with Mark paid for anyone who wants to square the pennies.
  • Searching the tour list finds archived tours too.
  • A tour no longer appears twice in the list. A tour stored in two documents was counted twice and the list kept loading “more” of the same tour.
  • Very old tours can be saved again. A tour from 2022 had no saved-state marker, so every save looked like somebody else had saved first and was refused forever.
  • The line under the tour name says which kind of trouble it was: no answer, the tour is not on the server, the server refused the change, or the server is in trouble — instead of “the server did not answer” for all four.

20 September 2026

  • The installed app opens from what the phone already has. Opening it after a while could show “Starting…” and give up: the saved copy was there but never used, so everything was downloaded again over a sleepy mobile connection. Now a slow network gets 2.5 seconds, and then the saved copy opens while the new one loads in the background.
  • The header says whether this browser has the current version, and one click fetches it when it does not.
  • Escape closes a form, and closing a new one does not lose it. Checking something elsewhere — who paid last time, how the hotel is spelled — meant closing the form and typing it all again. An unsaved new expense or person now waits on the device for two hours and comes back in the next blank form, with an offer to start blank instead.
  • The People tab keeps what you opened and typed when somebody else's edit arrives.
  • The tour list is newest first again, and says what it is sorted by.
  • Logging out forgets this device's copies of your tours; a login that merely expired keeps them.
  • Long lists load in pages from the database, so a large account's list and a tour's history open quickly.

18–19 September 2026

  • Tapping a notification opens the tour it is about, even when the app is already open somewhere else.
  • Switching between Full and Mini keeps you on the tab you were reading.
  • A person who still pays for, or shares, an expense cannot be deleted. It used to delete the expenses they paid for and turn “only for them” into “for everybody”. Now it says which expenses hold them.
  • An expired login asks for the code again instead of failing quietly; a deleted tour stays deleted rather than being brought back by a waiting edit.
  • The list says when edits are waiting for a tour it does not show, and links to it.
  • The compact People view is remembered for each tour on this device; it used to switch off with every edit.

16–17 September 2026

  • An open tour notices when somebody else changes it, and shows the change without a reload; changed rows light up for a moment. How often it checks is a setting.
  • Edits made offline are sent as soon as the network is back, wherever in the app you are, not only on the next edit.
  • An expense unfolds to show who it is for and how much each person carries — in Full and in Mini.
  • An expense for some of the people is shared by weight again.
  • Two expenses that had the same id are two expenses again. Saving one of them — without changing anything — overwrote the other.
  • Notifications read like a person wrote them, and the tour list rings the bell for tours this device follows.

13–15 September 2026

  • Help and Settings have a way back to the tour you came from, instead of the list and then the tour again.
  • Paybacks look like paybacks: green, and transfers inside a family cyan, in both lists, as in the old app.
  • The expense form and adding a person work the way they did in the old app.
  • Saving an expense no longer forgets what you were looking at.
  • The app can be installed on a phone's home screen as an app with its own icon, and says plainly when a browser will not allow it.
  • A page that cannot start says so and offers a reload, instead of staying blank.
  • The beta has a “b” on its icon, so the beta and the real one are not confused on a home screen.

10–11 September 2026

  • Mini: the same tours in a dense interface, one line per thing, for small screens and long lists.
  • Explain: every figure opens into how it was worked out — what was added, what was left out and why. Always on.
  • Stats with a chart you can click into, by category and by person.
  • Tour settings, currencies, history with restore, Mark paid, cloning, copying a tour as JSON, and two settings: the small-debt threshold and the accent colour.
  • A tour opens where it is needed: on the balances while it is being settled up, on the expenses otherwise.
  • Moving between screens no longer reloads the app — about seven times faster.
  • This help page.

8–9 September 2026 — the rewrite

  • The same app, rewritten in Rust. The arithmetic was rebuilt first and checked against the old app tour by tour, to the cent; then the server, which the old app could not tell from its own; then this interface.
  • One calculation everywhere. The screen, the server, the text pages and the explanations all use the same code, so they cannot disagree.
  • About eleven times smaller to download than the old app — 360 KB instead of 4 MB.
  • Works offline. Edits made without a network are kept on the device and sent later; if somebody else changed the tour meanwhile, both sets of changes are kept rather than one refused.
  • Two people saving at once can no longer both “win”: one save is refused and re-applied on top of the other.
  • A plain-text version at /t for browsers without JavaScript and for very bad connections, and push notifications.
